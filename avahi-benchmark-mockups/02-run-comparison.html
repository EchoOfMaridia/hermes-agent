FOR AVAHI The Open-Vend curve is the most diagnostic single visualization in the suite. A model that survives the full 28 days but ends under $500 is a strategy failure. A model that ends above $500 is a strategy win. A model that goes bankrupt is a tool-call cadence failure (typically: forgot collect_money, forgot restock, or hit 7 days unpaid). Three different failure modes, three different debugging paths. TPipe's own curve will be the gold line that ends at the top of the chart on the same seed.

FOR AVAHI General Concepts says: "we should resort to secondary values whenever possible for settling tiebreakers." A p-value column on the comparison table is the right answer. Mann-Whitney U for non-normal turn distributions, Welch's t for the profit curves. The dashboard should auto-flag rows where p > 0.05 as needing a tiebreaker (turn count, profit, accuracy subscore). n=5 is the minimum; the General Concepts doc says "decided based on whether we feel we need more than 5 to capture accurate results" — the column is also where Avahi adds a "n=" indicator so reviewers can see when we're underpowered.
